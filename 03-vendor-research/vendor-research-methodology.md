# Vendor and Account Research Methodology

## Research workflow

### Step 1: Understand the vendor
- Read the homepage and product overview.
- Review product documentation and use cases.
- Identify the problem category and core technical primitives.
- Read deployment and architecture material.
- Review public repositories or engineering content where useful.

### Step 2: Define the ICP
- Identify workloads that benefit from the product.
- Identify relevant technical environments.
- Map users, champions, evaluators, and economic buyers.
- Create qualification and disqualification criteria.

### Step 3: Build an account list
- Start with companies whose products imply multi-step workflows.
- Look for engineering hiring, product launches, integrations, and architecture content.
- Score fit separately from buying intent.
- Select a small number for deeper research.

### Step 4: Investigate one account
- Review official product pages and engineering blogs first.
- Use credible interviews, company announcements, and technical talks as supporting evidence.
- Capture exact evidence, source, date, and confidence.
- Avoid inferring private architecture from public product descriptions.

### Step 5: Form a discovery hypothesis
- Describe a plausible workflow.
- Identify a failure or coordination boundary.
- Explain possible operational and business impact.
- State what is unknown.
- Turn unknowns into neutral discovery questions.

### Step 6: Map buyers and write outreach
- Identify likely owners based on public role information.
- Match the message to the persona's responsibilities.
- Use one relevant technical observation.
- Ask a question that invites correction.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified:** Explicitly supported by a credible public source.
- **Reasonable inference:** A cautious interpretation of public evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** A possible internal condition that must be validated.
- **Unknown:** No reliable evidence available.

## Research quality checklist

- Is the claim directly supported by a source?
- Is the source official or otherwise credible?
- Is the information current?
- Have facts and assumptions been separated?
- Does the outreach avoid claiming knowledge of private systems?
- Is there a clear reason this account is worth contacting?
