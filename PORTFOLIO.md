# Restate Technical SDR/BDR Portfolio

## Executive summary

This portfolio demonstrates an end-to-end technical SDR workflow for a developer-infrastructure product. I selected Restate as the vendor, developed an understanding of durable execution, defined a target customer profile, researched Ramp as a potential account, mapped a plausible workflow risk, identified relevant buying personas, and designed a coordinated outbound campaign.

The central commercial idea is straightforward:

> When an application runs long-lived workflows across services, APIs, AI calls, and human approval steps, developers must manage state, retries, recovery, and side effects. Durable execution infrastructure can reduce the amount of custom orchestration machinery teams need to build and maintain.

The account exercise focuses on Ramp's public accounting automation and Ramp Stack context. The technical scenario is deliberately framed as a question to validate—not as an assertion that Ramp has experienced a particular failure.

## Work completed

### 1. Product intelligence
- Categorized Restate as durable execution and workflow infrastructure.
- Explained the problem space: long-running workflows, failure recovery, durable state, retries, timers, and coordination.
- Translated capabilities into engineering and business outcomes.
- Developed a balanced competitor discussion, including Temporal and cloud-native workflow options.

### 2. ICP definition
- Defined company and workload characteristics that may indicate fit.
- Identified technical, organizational, and trigger signals.
- Created qualification questions and disqualifying / low-priority signals.

### 3. Vendor research
- Established a target-account selection framework.
- Documented a repeatable research method that separates public evidence from assumptions.

### 4. Account research
- Selected Ramp for a focused account exercise.
- Documented public signals relevant to accounting automation and agentic workflows.
- Created an evidence register to make confidence and source quality visible.

### 5. Technical pain mapping
- Mapped a hypothetical accounting workflow involving reconciliation, approval, and an external journal-entry operation.
- Explored a failure window in which an external system may commit a write while the caller loses the response.
- Connected technical risk to reconciliation effort, customer trust, and operational overhead.
- Clarified where durable execution helps and where application-level idempotency remains necessary.

### 6. Buyer intelligence
- Mapped likely users, champions, technical evaluators, operational decision-makers, and economic buyers.
- Tailored value hypotheses and discovery questions by persona.

### 7. Multi-threaded outbound
- Created distinct messaging for a hands-on engineering champion and a senior technical evaluator.
- Prepared thoughtful responses to common objections without making unsupported competitor claims.

### 8. Sequencing
- Built a ten-day, multi-channel sequence.
- Included coordination rules, stop conditions, and personalization requirements.

## What this portfolio demonstrates

- Technical product comprehension without presenting myself as the product engineer.
- Ability to translate infrastructure concepts into business impact.
- Evidence-led account research.
- Hypothesis-driven discovery rather than pretending to know a prospect's internal architecture.
- Persona-specific messaging and coordinated outbound planning.
- Awareness of technical limitations, especially external side effects and idempotency.

## Important qualification

This is a simulated sales exercise. It is not evidence of a commercial relationship with Restate or Ramp, nor does it claim that Ramp is a Restate customer or has a confirmed need for the product.
