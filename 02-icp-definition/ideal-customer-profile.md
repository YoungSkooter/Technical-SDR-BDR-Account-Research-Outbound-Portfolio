# Ideal Customer Profile: Restate

## ICP thesis

A strong potential customer is a software company building long-running, multi-step backend or AI workflows where reliable progress, retries, waiting, and coordination are important—and where the engineering team is experiencing meaningful complexity maintaining those behaviors.

## Company characteristics

Potentially attractive segments:
- B2B SaaS platforms with complex business workflows.
- Fintech and financial automation companies.
- AI application and agent-platform companies.
- Data platforms and developer tools.
- Enterprise software with multi-system processes.

A practical initial prospecting band may be approximately 50–1,000 employees, but company size is a prioritization aid, not a product requirement. Smaller companies can be good fits when workflow complexity and technical urgency are high.

## Technical environment indicators

Signals worth investigating:
- Distributed services or microservice architecture.
- Cloud infrastructure and containerized workloads.
- Kubernetes, Docker, or serverless execution.
- Multiple external API integrations.
- Event-driven processing, queues, or asynchronous jobs.
- AI agents with multiple tool calls or long-running tasks.
- Human approval steps inside automated workflows.
- Hiring for platform engineering, backend infrastructure, AI infrastructure, or distributed systems.

These signals indicate possible technical relevance; none proves a need for Restate.

## Pain indicators

High-value discovery areas:
- Custom retry and recovery logic is growing.
- Workflow state is spread across queues, databases, and services.
- Long-running jobs fail or restart in difficult-to-debug ways.
- External side effects need careful idempotency handling.
- Human approvals interrupt otherwise automated processes.
- Teams are building internal workflow abstractions.
- Engineers spend time maintaining orchestration instead of product features.

## Likely users and buyers

- Users: backend engineers, AI engineers, platform engineers.
- Champions: Staff/Principal Engineers, senior backend engineers, AI infrastructure leads.
- Technical evaluators: platform leads, distributed-systems engineers, architects.
- Operational decision-makers: Engineering Managers and Directors.
- Economic buyers: VP Engineering, CTO, or a relevant product/engineering executive.

Actual roles vary by organization and deal size.
